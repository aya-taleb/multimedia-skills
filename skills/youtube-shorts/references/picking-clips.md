# Picking clips: worked example

Transcript lines (made up for this example):

```
[00:33.5] Here's the mistake almost every food business makes. You price for your first customer.
[00:41.8] But you should price for your last customer, the one with ten other options.
[00:50.2] If the price works for her, it works for everyone.
[00:59.0] We started at nine dollars a person, with a twenty person minimum.
```

A good clip card:

```
1. 00:33.5 to 00:58.7 (25 s)
   Stands alone: one complete idea, no setup needed.
   Opening line (as spoken): "Here's the mistake almost every food business makes."
   On-screen text (first 2 s): Price for your last customer
   Title: Price for your last customer, not your first
   Reframe: speaker centered in a wide shot; crop 9:16 on the speaker, no slide needed.
   Check: none.
```

Why the end is 00:58.7 and not 00:59.0: it stops at the end of the finished thought, just
before the next segment starts.

## Patterns that tend to stand alone

- A rule with its reason ("price for the last customer, because...").
- A mistake and what happened ("I gave discounts. Every office asked again.").
- A short before and after ("We used to..., now we...").
- A direct answer to a question from the Q&A, if the question is restated in the answer.

## Patterns that do not

- Housekeeping, screen-share chatter, "as I said earlier", "on this slide".
- A list where item one is in the clip and items two and three are not.
- Jokes that need the room.

## Cold open

When the strongest line comes second, start the clip on it and let the setup follow, or cut
the setup. Note it as: "Cold open: start at 00:41.8 ('But you should price for your last
customer'), then play 00:33.5 to 00:41.5." Only reorder whole sentences, never words, so the
speaker still says what they said.
