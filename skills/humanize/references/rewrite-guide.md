# How to fix each check by hand

The script cleans characters. These are the fixes that need a person, or you, to make a
call. Each one names the check as the score report does.

## Words on the avoid list

Swap the word for the plainer one the report suggests. Better: ask what the word was
standing in for, and say that. "A seamless checkout" becomes "checkout in two taps", if <!-- allow-words -->
the draft says two taps somewhere. If it doesn't, write "checkout that's quick" and
leave `{{how many steps}}` for the founder.

## Stock words and stock phrases

Most can be cut with no loss. Read the sentence without the phrase. If it still says the
same thing, the phrase was filler. "It's worth noting that we doubled sales" becomes "We
doubled sales."

## Stock opening or sign-off

Open with the most specific thing already in the draft: a number, a moment, a place, a
customer's words. Move it to the top. End with the real next step ("Reply with your
address and I'll send a sample") or one real question that only this reader can answer.
"Thoughts?" and "Agree?" are not real questions.

## The "it's not X, it's Y" move

Say what it is. "It's not a planner. It's a movement." becomes "It's a planner that
remembers what you didn't finish." Keep one of these at most, if it's the strongest line.

## Long sentences

Split at "and", "which", "because" or "so". One idea per sentence. Read it on a phone
width: if it wraps past three lines, it's too long.

## Every sentence the same length

Make one sentence very short. Two to four words. Let another one run a bit longer with a
detail. Reading out loud is the fastest test: flat rhythm sounds like reciting.

## Stacked one-word sentences

"Fast. Cheap. Done." Join them ("It's fast and cheap.") or keep the one that matters.

## Lists of three, over and over

Keep the best triple. Cut the others to two items, or swap them for one concrete example.

## Exclamation marks, emoji bullets, hashtag piles

One exclamation mark at most. Plain line breaks or hyphens instead of emoji at the start
of each line. Three hashtags or fewer, and only ones people search.

## Markdown symbols

Remove `**` and `#` for any platform that doesn't render them. On LinkedIn, put emphasis
in the words instead of in bold.

## Long paragraphs

Break after two or three sentences. On social posts, one or two sentences per paragraph
is normal.

## Hidden characters, long dashes, curly quotes

The script fixes these. If a long dash carried real meaning (a sharp turn in the
sentence), check the comma it became still reads right. Sometimes a period is better.
